The case for it
The most literal reading of what Hisab replaces, and the only mark that says ledger rather than money. The spine gives it an asymmetry that survives masking — it never looks like a plain rounded square.
What it costs
A book is the single most common icon metaphor on any app store. Next to a notes app, a reader and a dictionary, this is the one a shop owner might tap by mistake.

Unmistakably about money, and unmistakably Bangladeshi — the ৳ is not a currency mark anyone confuses. Strongest of the four at 48px, because it is one bold closed form with nothing to lose.

Generic in the category. Every wallet, every payment app and every bank in the country has a claim on this symbol, so it says money without saying Hisab.

The case for it
A column of figures ending in a heavier total — the shape of the one thing the product is for. Nothing else on a home screen looks like it, and it scales down better than any drawing because it is only three bars.
What it costs
Abstract, and DESIGN.md tells the product to draw literally. A first-time user learns nothing from it; it earns meaning only after they have used the app.

The case for it
Bangla on the home screen, before the app is even opened — the clearest possible signal that this product is in the owner's language. A single letter reads at any size, and it is the only one of the four that is ours rather than a category convention.
What it costs
Meaningless to anyone who does not read Bangla, which includes app-store reviewers and any future market. And হ alone is not distinctive — several Bangla words start with it.
B and D are drawn with the live Hind Siliguri face so they render here. A shipped icon cannot depend on a font — whichever wins, the glyph gets traced to outlines and then redrawn by hand, because a type designer's ৳ or হ is optimised for a paragraph, not for 48 pixels.
